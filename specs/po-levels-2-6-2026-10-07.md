# PO level design for Levels 2–6 (verbatim, 2026-10-07)

Source of truth for the campaign redesign. The epic files `epic-20` … `epic-24` are derived from this text; where they disagree, this file wins.

---

## Level 2: The Failed Rescue

Level 2 starts inside the fire department, directly after Level 1. A few hours have passed, so it is now around midday. The lighting should clearly feel different from Level 1, with a higher sun, harder light and changed shadows.

The fire department initially feels relatively safe. The player can walk around for roughly 20 to 30 seconds. There is no combat yet. Firefighters move around, radio chatter plays, injured or frightened civilians may be present, and the player gets a brief feeling that organized help still exists.

An emergency call comes in. A red or blue alarm light activates, the station alarm sounds and the firefighters immediately move.

Before leaving, the player can optionally take a fire axe from the station. The axe is stronger than the baseball bat from Level 1.

The axe behaves differently from the bat:

- The baseball bat primarily attacks one enemy at a time.
- The axe is stronger.
- If only one enemy is close, the player performs a normal axe attack.
- If several zombies are surrounding the player, the same attack automatically becomes a circular roundhouse-style swing, hitting multiple nearby enemies and creating space.

The player then leaves with a group of roughly six firefighters in a fire truck.

The firefighters are not invulnerable NPCs. Once they leave the vehicle, they behave much more like armed pedestrians. They can fight and help the player, but zombies can attack, bite and infect them just like anybody else.

### Rescue scene

The fire truck arrives at a rescue location.

There are approximately 30 civilians trapped inside a building. Initially there are no zombies directly in the rescue area.

The player can immediately move around after leaving the truck. The firefighters go to the building and begin opening the doors.

The timing is important:

1. Firefighters arrive.
2. Everyone exits the truck.
3. Firefighters move toward the trapped civilians.
4. They open the doors.
5. Around 30 civilians begin leaving the building.
6. At exactly that moment, zombies appear from the surrounding streets and edges of the area.

This should feel like a very short moment of success that instantly collapses into chaos.

The civilians panic and try to escape. The firefighters fight back. The player can help.

The entire scene should use the same outbreak simulation rules established in Level 1:

- zombies attack visible humans
- pedestrians flee
- bitten humans turn
- firefighters can turn as well
- zombies choose nearby visible targets
- the situation can snowball naturally

In the worst case, the player could eventually be surrounded by something close to:

- 30 former civilians turned infected
- 6 infected firefighters
- additional zombies that originally entered the scene

The player is not expected to kill all of them.

The intended gameplay is:

fight a little → retreat → break away → kill the few that follow → continue escaping

The player can theoretically brute-force the encounter through repeated deaths and respawns, but surviving normally should require disengaging.

The axe makes the player stronger than in Level 1, but the size of the outbreak increases much faster than the player's power.

The main lesson of the level is:

You can kill zombies. You cannot kill the outbreak.

### Second phase

The rescue scene itself should happen quickly, roughly within the first part of the level. It is not enough to fill the full five-minute experience.

After the rescue collapses, a radio message gives the player a new objective: reach a temporary evacuation point.

The rest of the level becomes an escape through nearby streets.

There should now be many zombies spread across the map, but they should not all magically know where the player is. They continue following the perception rules from Level 1.

The player has to:

- move through streets
- avoid larger groups
- use line of sight
- run when necessary
- fight isolated zombies or small groups
- use environmental distractions where possible
- keep moving toward the evacuation point

Near the end, there can be one particularly dense cluster blocking the obvious route. The intended solution is not necessarily to clear the entire group. The player should create or find an opening and get through.

### End point

The escape destination is a police checkpoint at a bridge.

The checkpoint should visually communicate that some organized control still exists:

- police vehicles
- lights
- barricades
- officers
- gates
- civilians behind the line

The player crosses the checkpoint.

The gate closes behind them.

This ends Level 2 and transitions directly into Level 3.

### Environment and atmosphere

The city is still recognizable and mostly intact, but several hours of outbreak have passed.

Compared with Level 1, add early signs of damage:

- a few corpses
- abandoned vehicles
- crashed cars
- trash cans knocked over
- scattered belongings
- some broken windows
- perhaps one smoking vehicle
- small signs of previous fighting
- isolated blood trails
- police and emergency activity

It should not yet look post-apocalyptic.

The feeling should be:

The city is wounded, but still standing.

## Level 3: The Police Collapse

Level 3 begins at the police-controlled area reached at the end of Level 2.

The level should immediately feel more action-heavy and more powerful than the previous two levels.

The player starts around police officers who are organized, armed and capable of fighting.

### New weapon: handgun

Early in the level, the player can obtain a gun.

The gun is the first proper ranged weapon.

Compared with the axe:

- it attacks at distance
- it is useful against enemies before they reach the player
- it is mainly a single-target weapon
- it does not replace the axe's crowd-clearing advantage

The player can still use previously obtained weapons such as the baseball bat or axe.

### Police station attack

Shortly after the player equips themselves, the police position is attacked by zombies.

Police officers defend the area with guns.

The player fights alongside them.

Police officers can also be bitten and infected. They are helpful, but not protected characters.

The attack becomes too large to hold.

The player has to leave.

A small group of police officers, roughly three or four, comes with the player.

### Main structure

Level 3 should be more linear than Levels 1 and 2.

Instead of a roughly square neighborhood map, it should feel like forward movement through the city.

Use approximately three parallel streets leading in the same general direction.

The player can switch between them, but must keep moving forward.

This gives some route choice without turning the level into free exploration.

The police officers travel with the player and automatically fight zombies they encounter.

At first, having armed police nearby should make the player feel unusually safe.

Then the number of zombies increases.

Over time, the officers may be:

- separated
- overwhelmed
- bitten
- killed
- turned into infected

The group gradually falls apart.

This creates the progression:

strong squad → dangerous journey → squad gets reduced → player increasingly alone

### Health recovery

Because this level contains significantly more combat, place medkits along the route.

The player should be able to recover health by finding and taking them.

The level should remain action-heavy without becoming an unavoidable attrition death.

### Environment

Level 3 takes place later in the day, toward late afternoon.

Use:

- longer shadows
- more smoke
- harsher light
- warmer, more dramatic atmosphere

The city is now visibly beginning to collapse.

The three streets can each show a different type of damage:

Residential street

- damaged gardens
- abandoned houses
- crashed cars
- makeshift barricades
- blood and bodies

Shopping street

- broken shop windows
- looted stores
- scattered merchandise
- abandoned bags
- damaged signs

Main road

- wrecked buses
- abandoned police vehicles
- traffic jams
- stronger signs of fighting

There should be noticeably more:

- fires
- corpses
- wreckage
- broken glass
- abandoned vehicles

But the city should still be recognizable as a functioning place that has only recently collapsed.

### End point

The player eventually reaches a much stronger army checkpoint.

This should feel like an escalation from the police checkpoint.

There are:

- soldiers
- heavier barricades
- military vehicles
- buses filled with civilians
- organized evacuation activity

The player crosses into the army-controlled zone.

The level ends there.

This sets up Level 4.

## Level 4: The Safe City Falls

Level 4 begins with the player already inside an evacuation bus or convoy.

The army is transporting civilians away from the collapsing city toward another city that has not yet been hit by the outbreak.

This level should begin as a deliberate false reset.

### Arrival

The convoy reaches the new city.

Everything looks good again.

The player sees:

- clean streets
- intact buildings
- pedestrians
- functioning shops
- normal infrastructure
- organized army presence

For a moment, it should feel like the player has actually escaped.

### New weapon: machine gun

Because the player is now with the army, they receive a machine gun.

The machine gun becomes the strongest weapon so far.

It differs from the handgun because it can deal with several enemies quickly and is suitable for larger groups.

There is no ammunition system in the game. The machine gun has effectively unlimited ammunition.

The player can use the machine gun as their main weapon during this level.

### The outbreak starts again

Do not attack the convoy immediately.

Let the player experience the safe city first.

Then, while civilians are unloading or gathering, one or more evacuees turn.

The infection spreads among the crowd.

The cruel twist is that the evacuation has brought the outbreak into a city that was previously safe.

### Scale

This should be the first true large-scale war scene.

Use something roughly like:

- around 20 soldiers
- around 50 civilians
- rapidly increasing infected numbers

Many of the civilians should now also be armed.

By this point in the outbreak, people understand what is happening.

Some civilians have:

- pistols
- hunting rifles
- improvised weapons

They fight back rather than simply running helplessly.

The battle therefore becomes a huge mixed scene:

- soldiers firing
- armed civilians fighting
- unarmed civilians panicking
- people being bitten
- newly infected turning
- zombies charging from multiple directions

### Military spectacle

This level should visually escalate into something close to a war zone.

Include military elements such as:

- heavy machine guns
- military vehicles
- perhaps tanks
- perhaps helicopters
- soldiers holding defensive lines
- intense gunfire

The player should feel powerful with the machine gun.

But the level should simultaneously demonstrate that even this level of firepower cannot contain the infection.

The key feeling is:

Everyone is fighting, and it is still not enough.

### Ending

The city collapses into complete chaos.

The player eventually reaches a basement or underground room.

This is not an official safe zone.

It is simply somewhere the player can hide.

The player goes inside alone and shuts themselves in.

The battle continues outside.

The level ends with the player hiding while the war above continues.

This directly sets up Level 5.

## Level 5: Aftermath

Level 5 uses the same city and essentially the same area as Level 4.

This reuse is intentional.

Several hours have passed.

It is now evening.

The sun is setting, giving the world a dark red, orange and increasingly gloomy appearance.

The contrast with Level 4 should be extreme.

Level 4 was deafening.

Level 5 is almost silent.

### Opening

The player emerges from the basement.

The same streets from Level 4 are now empty.

There are:

- no active soldiers
- no large civilian crowds
- no active battle
- no helicopters fighting
- no organized resistance

The battle is over.

The environment shows what happened.

### Environment

The city should now look genuinely devastated.

Use the same recognizable buildings from Level 4, but heavily damaged.

Examples:

- burned-out cars
- wrecked military vehicles
- fires still burning
- broken windows
- collapsed or damaged facades
- bullet damage
- smoke
- corpses
- scattered weapons and belongings
- destroyed barricades

The buildings should still clearly be the same buildings.

The impact comes from recognizing places the player saw intact or active in the previous level.

The world should feel like the aftermath of a war.

### Zombies

There are still infected around, but not huge hordes.

A few zombies wander through the ruined streets.

They are dangerous enough to maintain tension, but the level is deliberately not another mass battle.

### Main mission: find five survivors

The player hears or discovers signs that other people may still be alive.

The objective becomes:

Find five survivors.

They are hiding in different places around the city.

The player moves through the devastated environment and finds them one by one.

Each survivor then follows the player.

The machine gun remains the player's primary weapon.

There is no need for a new weapon in this level.

### Escort gameplay

The challenge is protecting the survivor group.

The player is the only heavily armed protector.

Zombies are sparse, but even a single zombie reaching the group can be dangerous.

The group gradually grows:

1 survivor → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5

This makes every later encounter slightly more tense because there are more people to protect.

Not every survivor necessarily has to make it.

If somebody dies or becomes infected, the level continues.

### Destination

The survivors need to be taken to a temporary shelter.

The chosen destination is a subway station.

The subway station is defensible and has attracted other survivors.

The player leads whoever remains of the five survivors into the station.

The level ends there.

## Level 6: The Subway

Level 6 begins inside the subway station.

The survivors rescued in Level 5 are present, or however many of them actually survived.

The station has also attracted around 20 to 30 additional survivors.

For a moment, this should feel comparatively pleasant.

People are:

- sitting
- talking
- treating injuries
- resting
- sharing supplies

The player can walk around and absorb the atmosphere.

There is no new weapon at the beginning of this level.

Weapon escalation should pause here.

The machine gun is already powerful enough, and adding another bigger weapon would feel artificial.

The gameplay escalation now comes from responsibility rather than firepower.

### Breach

The subway shelter does not remain safe.

Infected begin getting inside.

The preferred direction is through maintenance tunnels or other underground access routes, rather than simply repeating an above-ground street attack.

At first:

- one or two infected appear
- the problem feels manageable

Then more arrive.

The shelter begins to fail.

### Evacuation through the subway

The survivors have to abandon the station and move deeper into the subway system.

The player becomes the person covering the retreat.

Gameplay revolves around:

- protecting civilians
- controlling chokepoints
- holding doors or narrow passages
- shooting infected while survivors move
- protecting stragglers
- moving the group through tunnels

The confined subway environment should make the level feel very different from the open streets of Levels 1 to 5.

The player has strong firepower, but a large vulnerable group to protect.

### Ending

The group eventually reaches another subway exit.

They emerge above ground.

It is now night.

That is the endpoint for the currently designed sequence and provides the starting point for Level 7 later.

## Escalation across Levels 2 to 6

The overall progression should feel very deliberate:

- Level 2: Firefighters still respond. Emergency services are failing.
- Level 3: Police fight openly. The city is collapsing.
- Level 4: The army enters the fight. A supposedly safe second city falls into full-scale warfare.
- Level 5: The war is over. The player walks through its silent aftermath and searches for survivors.
- Level 6: The survivors gather underground, but even the subway is eventually breached.

The environmental progression is just as important:

Midday → late afternoon → false safety → sunset devastation → underground shelter → night.

And the player's relationship to other people evolves too:

rescued by firefighters → fight alongside police → fight alongside an army → become the primary protector of survivors.
