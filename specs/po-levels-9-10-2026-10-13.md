# PO campaign update — Level 9 modification + Level 10 "The Cure" (2026-10-13, verbatim)

> Source of truth for L9 and L10. Wins over epic-31 and over `po-levels-4-9-2026-10-11.md` on any disagreement about L9. Orchestrator decisions are in the section at the end.

Level 9: Full Modification

Core change

Level 9 no longer allows the armored vehicle to break down permanently before the end.

The player keeps the armored military vehicle for the entire level.

The vehicle still has a damage bar, and hitting zombies still damages it, but it should never be allowed to fail before the scripted ending. The damage system remains useful because it encourages the player to avoid unnecessary collisions instead of simply mowing down everything.

The key rule becomes:

The car can be damaged throughout Level 9, but it survives until the final scripted crash.

Level 9 flow

The level starts directly after the zoo escape.

The player is inside an armored military vehicle and has to cross a very large open area toward the next city.

This should feel different from all previous levels:

- much larger space
- long open roads
- countryside
- dry terrain
- optional off-road routes
- scattered buildings or abandoned infrastructure
- many zombies wandering across the area

There are no mounted guns.

The vehicle's only offensive capability is running zombies over.

Every collision damages the vehicle.

Zombies can also damage the vehicle if the player slows down or becomes surrounded.

The player therefore has to constantly choose between:

- driving around groups
- taking longer but safer routes
- pushing through smaller groups
- accepting vehicle damage to save time

The level should reward careful driving rather than pure destruction.

Vehicle damage

The vehicle has a visible damage bar.

Damage comes from:

- hitting zombies
- hitting obstacles
- zombies attacking the vehicle
- collisions with wreckage

The damage bar does not regenerate.

However, for Level 9 specifically, reaching zero should not cause a normal breakdown.

Instead, the car remains barely functional until the finale.

It can visually deteriorate:

- dents
- cracked glass
- smoke
- damaged lights
- engine sounds becoming worse

But the player can always continue.

Level 9 ending

The player finally approaches the outskirts of the next major location.

At this point the level triggers a forced crash.

This can involve:

- a collapsed road
- destroyed bridge section
- explosion
- falling debris
- wrecked vehicles blocking the road
- a sudden impact while avoiding infected

The armored vehicle is destroyed.

This is not optional.

The player survives the crash.

The important narrative purpose is to reset the player's equipment.

During the crash:

- the vehicle is lost
- all weapons collected throughout the previous campaign are lost
- the player is separated from the equipment
- the player starts Level 10 on foot

The player should retain only the axe as the most basic surviving weapon.

Level 9 ends immediately after the crash or after the player crawls away from the wreck.

This creates the clean handover into the finale:

Level 9 ends with the player stripped back to almost nothing.

---

Level 10: The Cure

Level 10 is the final level and should be much larger than every previous level.

Target duration:

approximately 45 to 50 minutes

This is not another five-minute snackable level. It is the full finale.

The player starts alone, on foot, with only the axe.

The entire level is a long journey through a huge mixed environment toward the final cure dispersal system.

The level should feel like everything in the game coming together.

Core story

A cure exists.

Not a vaccine and not something that saves infected people.

It is an airborne agent that is harmless to healthy humans but immediately kills infected humans and infected animals.

The player's objective is to:

1. survive the crash
2. rebuild the arsenal
3. reach the research facility
4. obtain the cure
5. carry it across the level
6. reach the dispersal system
7. activate it
8. survive the final attack while the system charges
9. kill the Mega Alpha
10. release the cure
11. watch the infection finally collapse

For the first time in the entire game, the situation actually reverses.

Until this level, the outbreak always gets worse.

In Level 10, the player finally stops it.

---

Level 10 Structure

The level should consist of several visually distinct regions rather than one generic city.

It should feel like a huge mixed military and research zone on the outskirts of civilization.

Possible progression:

Crash outskirts → residential fringe → industrial area → military zone → research campus → destroyed corridor → cure dispersal complex

There should be no skyscrapers.

The environments can be relatively low-rise but geographically large.

The world should feel bigger through variety and travel distance, not height.

---

Opening: Vulnerable Again

The player wakes up after the Level 9 crash.

The armored vehicle is destroyed.

The player's full arsenal is gone.

The player only has the axe.

There should initially be only a few zombies.

This is important.

The player has gone from being extremely powerful in Levels 7 to 9 back to vulnerability.

The first section should force the player to:

- run
- avoid groups
- use the axe carefully
- break line of sight
- move intelligently

The level should gradually increase enemy numbers as stronger weapons are recovered.

---

Weapon Progression

Level 10 should rebuild the player's arsenal step by step.

Each weapon should correspond to a meaningful location or checkpoint.

The exact order can follow increasing power.

Example progression:

Checkpoint 1: Axe

Starting weapon.

Only small numbers of infected.

Checkpoint 2: Handgun

Found at a police or security position.

The number of infected increases.

Checkpoint 3: Shotgun

Found at a defended survivor position, hunting shop, security room or abandoned barricade.

Stronger infected begin appearing more frequently.

Checkpoint 4: Machine gun

Recovered from a destroyed military position.

Large groups now become common.

Midpoint: Cure

The player reaches the research facility.

This is a major story checkpoint.

The player obtains the cure canister or cure delivery system.

From this point onward, the player's primary mission changes:

Get the cure to the dispersal facility.

Checkpoint 5: Rocket launcher

Found late in the level, before the final facility.

The player needs it specifically because of the Mega Alpha.

Final weapon: Mounted rotary minigun

At the cure dispersal site there is a fixed heavy rotary gun.

It has:

- unlimited ammunition
- extreme damage against regular infected
- strong crowd-clearing power
- an overheating mechanic

It is the most powerful sustained-fire weapon in the game.

However, it should not be sufficient by itself to kill the Mega Alpha.

---

Enemy Scaling

Enemy density should rise as the player's arsenal increases.

The level should follow roughly this logic:

Axe → few zombies

Handgun → more zombies

Shotgun → stronger individual enemies

Machine gun → larger groups

Rocket launcher → major hordes and multiple Alphas

Minigun → final mass assault

The increase should feel natural rather than like enemies simply spawning because the player found a weapon.

The world itself becomes more dangerous as the player gets deeper into the infected zone.

---

Infected Dogs

Level 10 introduces infected dogs.

The player's own corgi remains completely safe and is never infected.

Other dogs in the world can be infected.

Infected dogs should be:

- fast
- low to the ground
- difficult to track in crowds
- dangerous when the player is distracted
- visually distinct from normal infected humans

They add a threat that feels different without requiring another giant monster.

They fit especially well in the early and middle sections where the player has fewer crowd-control weapons.

---

The Mega Alpha

The Mega Alpha is the final boss.

It should be present throughout almost the entire level.

It is not a boss that suddenly spawns at the end.

The player is being hunted by it from very early in the level.

Behavior

The Mega Alpha always knows approximately where the player is.

Unlike normal zombies, it does not rely on normal sight rules.

It constantly follows the player.

However, its normal movement speed is only around 70% of the player's running speed.

This means the player can stay ahead of it.

The player can never permanently escape it.

It is always coming.

Close-range behavior

When the Mega Alpha gets close enough, it can perform sudden high-speed attacks.

These should make close contact extremely dangerous.

The idea is:

Far away: slow, inevitable pursuit

Close: explosive and deadly

Audio

Because the game uses a bird's-eye view, the player often cannot see the Mega Alpha from far away.

Therefore it needs a unique audio identity.

The player should periodically hear:

- a distinctive deep roar
- distant impacts
- heavy footsteps when closer
- increasingly loud vocalizations

The sound tells the player:

It is still coming.

The closer it gets, the louder and more threatening the audio becomes.

This should create constant pressure across the entire 45 to 50 minute level.

The player should never feel completely safe.

Combat

Normal weapons cannot realistically kill the Mega Alpha.

They can:

- slow it
- stagger it
- temporarily create distance

But they should not finish it.

The player needs the rocket launcher.

It should take roughly 10 direct rocket hits to kill the Mega Alpha.

This makes the final fight substantial without turning it into an abstract health sponge.

---

Regular Alphas

Normal Alphas from Level 4 onward still exist.

They appear throughout the later sections of Level 10.

They are much stronger than standard infected, but unlike the Mega Alpha they can be killed with conventional weapons.

The player therefore has to distinguish between:

- regular infected
- infected dogs
- regular Alphas
- Mega Alpha

---

Research Facility and Cure

Around the middle of the level, the player reaches the research campus.

This is where the cure was developed.

The facility can contain:

- low modern laboratory buildings
- medical tents
- abandoned equipment
- emergency generators
- research vehicles
- quarantine areas

The player discovers the airborne cure.

This can be explained through:

- a surviving scientist
- recorded message
- terminal
- radio transmission

The important information is simple:

The cure works, but it must be released through a large regional ventilation or dispersal system.

The player takes the cure.

From here onward, the player is carrying the single most important object in the game.

---

Second Half: Deliver the Cure

The destination is a huge cure dispersal complex.

It should feel visually unique and worthy of the finale.

Possible elements:

- giant ventilation fans
- large pipes
- turbines
- pressure tanks
- filtration towers
- military infrastructure
- industrial platforms

It can feel almost cathedral-like in scale despite remaining low-rise.

The player must fight through increasingly dense infected areas to reach it.

The Mega Alpha continues following.

The player cannot simply sprint to the destination because each section still contains obstacles, enemies and weapon checkpoints.

---

Why the Mega Alpha Must Be Killed

Until the finale, the Mega Alpha does not need to be killed.

Running away is valid.

At the dispersal facility, this changes.

The player installs the cure and starts the release sequence.

The system needs approximately 60 seconds to:

- pressurize
- activate the ventilation network
- prepare the airborne agent

The player cannot leave.

If the player abandons the system, the cure will not be released.

The Mega Alpha reaches the facility during this countdown.

This is why the final boss fight is mandatory.

The player is no longer escaping.

The player has to stand their ground.

---

Final Battle

The player activates the dispersal system.

A visible countdown or system progress begins.

Large numbers of infected attack.

The player can use the mounted rotary minigun.

This allows the player to cut through huge numbers of standard infected.

The minigun:

- has unlimited ammo
- overheats
- destroys normal infected extremely quickly
- is very strong against regular Alphas

Then the Mega Alpha arrives.

The minigun can hurt and slow it, but cannot finish it efficiently.

The player must switch to the rocket launcher.

During the final sequence, the player is therefore balancing:

- minigun against the horde
- rocket launcher against Mega Alpha
- regular weapons when repositioning

The Mega Alpha requires approximately ten direct rocket hits.

The player finally kills it.

At roughly the same time, the dispersal system reaches full pressure.

---

Cure Release

The player activates the final release.

Huge ventilation systems turn on.

Fans begin spinning.

The airborne cure enters the environment as a visible cloud or mist.

The cure is harmless to uninfected humans.

Every infected entity touched by it dies immediately.

This includes:

- normal infected
- Alphas
- infected dogs
- infected zoo animals elsewhere
- any remaining infected types

They do not turn back into humans.

They simply collapse and die.

---

Playable Ending Sequence

After the cure is released, the game does not immediately cut to a cinematic.

The player keeps control.

Combat is over.

This should be a playable epilogue.

The player can walk around while the cure spreads.

They see:

- infected running toward them and suddenly collapsing
- Alphas falling
- distant groups dying
- survivors emerging from hiding
- streets becoming quiet
- ventilation systems continuing to pump the cure outward

The player's weapons are no longer necessary.

The world that has been increasingly dangerous for ten levels becomes safe in front of the player.

This should last long enough to enjoy the reversal.

Eventually the camera can gradually pull farther out.

The cloud spreads beyond the immediate map.

The player sees that the cure is reaching a much larger area.

After an entire game where the infection spreads outward, the final visual is the cure spreading outward instead.

Then:

Minor Incident

Credits.

The final emotional arc is:

Level 1: you accidentally help start the outbreak.

Level 10: you end it.

## Orchestrator decisions (2026-10-13, changeable)

- **L9 vehicle:** the shared APC keeps its durability bar and per-hit damage (lane l9-durability). In L9, 0 HP is a "barely functional" floor: top speed is capped to about 60%, there is heavy smoke and a wrecked engine sound, but no breakdown and no on-foot fallback. The damage visuals step through dents, cracked glass, smoke and dead lights at 75/50/25/0%. The L9-S2 breakdown/on-foot plan from epic-31 is cancelled. The scripted ending is a collapsed bridge or road section at the city outskirts: a cinematic crash, then a short crawl-away. The handover to L10 strips the loadout to `weapon.fire-axe` only (abilities such as the corgi lure stay; the corgi survives and stays safe).
- **L10 scale and performance:** 45–50 min. Six or seven streamed regions (crash outskirts, residential fringe, industrial area, military zone, research campus, destroyed corridor, dispersal complex), all low-rise, built on the L9 terrain streaming (L9-S3/S4). Region checkpoints double as respawn points (all-levels rule: respawn at the last checkpoint). The E18 budgets hold everywhere.
- **Weapons:** the player re-acquires them at region checkpoints: handgun (police or security post), shotgun (survivor barricade or hunting shop), machine gun (destroyed military post), rocket launcher (late, before the complex). The mounted rotary minigun at the complex uses the existing mounted-gun interaction, with unlimited ammo and an overheat bar.
- **Cure:** a canister carried as a backpack. It doesn't take a weapon slot and doesn't slow the player. If the player dies, it's restored at the last checkpoint. The research campus delivers the story through a recorded message plus a terminal (no new voice actor). The dispersal console has a 60 s charge with visible progress; it only progresses while the player stays within about 25 m.
- **Infected dogs:** a new `inf.dog` derived from a generic dog base (not the corgi), with fast, low-profile behaviour reusing the animal pipeline. The player's corgi is excluded from all infection paths.
- **Mega Alpha:** `inf.mega-alpha`, derived from the Alpha at about 2× its size. It always knows the player's position (no sight checks), cruises at 0.7× the player's run speed, and uses close-range burst attacks (charge, slam, grab). Its audio is a unique roar, distant impacts and footsteps whose loudness scales with distance. Normal weapons only slow or stagger it; about 10 direct rocket hits kill it. It shows on the minimap only as a pulsing distance ring, never as an exact dot.
- **Cure release and epilogue:** a mist VFX expands outward; every infected touched dies (no turning back). Survivors emerge, the player keeps control for about 2–3 min, the camera slowly pulls out, then the "Minor Incident" title and credits.
- **Order of work:** specs → L9 changes → L10 assets (Mega Alpha, infected dog, minigun, cure canister, region kits) → L10 regions → encounters and weapons → finale → epilogue.
